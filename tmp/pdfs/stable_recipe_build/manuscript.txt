011                                               A BSTRACT
012
013            Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) provides a general framework for
014            consolidating the capabilities of multiple teachers specialized through reinforce-
015
               ment learning into a single student model. However, it remains unclear how
               teacher supervision is weighted across tasks, how it shapes the resulting optimiza-
016
               tion dynamics, and whether different teachers induce overlapping parameter up-
017
               dates. Our analysis identifies two distinct effects of response length under token
018            averaging: changing the relative weights of domains and weighting responses un-
019            equally within each domain. Fixing domain weights removes only the first effect,
020            while equal response weighting also removes the second. Controlled comparisons
021            show that these choices change gradient direction and redistribute capability gains,
022            with global-token averaging favoring mathematics and equal response weighting
023            favoring instruction following in our experiments. We further find that joint train-
024            ing produces concentrated cumulative changes in BF16 model weights, while the
025            FP32 weights change more broadly. Local comparisons at a common student and
026
               optimizer state reveal similar update concentration across sampled-token, top-k,
               and full-vocabulary supervision, despite their different vocabulary coverage. On-
027
               line sampled-token and top-k training nevertheless develop different capability
028
               profiles across domains and checkpoints. These findings characterize how loss
029            averaging allocates teacher supervision and show why vocabulary coverage alone
030            is insufficient to characterize parameter-update concentration or capability trans-
031            fer.

034   1   I NTRODUCTION
035
036   A language model may need mathematical reasoning, code generation, scientific knowledge, and
037   instruction following within the same deployment. Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD)
038
      transfers such capabilities by having specialized teachers score responses generated by a shared
      student (Ma et al., 2026). Because the student supplies the responses, its current behavior determines
039
      both the examples on which teachers provide feedback and the lengths over which that feedback is
040
      averaged.
041
042   Equal numbers of prompts from different domains need not give those domains equal weight in
043   the loss. Open-MOPD identifies response-length imbalance as a source of unequal supervision (Gao
044
      et al., 2026). This raises a more specific question about the objective: does an averaging rule balance
      domains, responses within each domain, or both? These choices can be separated algebraically and
045
      compared empirically through the capabilities of the resulting student.
046
047   The parameter changes induced by teacher feedback raise a second question. Prior work finds con-
048   centrated changes in reinforcement learning (Mukherjee et al., 2025) and distillation (Yu et al.,
049   2026; Shen et al., 2026). In a shared student, each teacher acts on parameters already influenced
050
      by other teachers. Concentration alone does not specify whether teachers affect the same locations.
      The relevant comparison therefore begins with a common student and optimizer state, and relates
051
      teacher-specific parameter changes to differences between teacher predictions.
052
053   A third question concerns the breadth of teacher feedback. A sampled-token loss and a loss over
      several vocabulary alternatives supervise the same generated prefix differently. Their vocabulary

054
      coverage does not directly determine how many model parameters change: the softmax couples
055   logits, and the optimizer combines the current gradient with its history. Comparing both update
056   concentration and capability connects the local learning signal to training outcomes.
057
058
      We organize the paper around these three questions:
059
            1. Token balancing (Section 3). We derive how global-token, domain-token, and domain-
060            response averaging weight domains and response lengths. The experiments show how
061            these choices redistribute capability gains across domains.
062
            2. Update sparsity and teacher overlap (Section 4). We measure concentration of joint
063
               parameter changes, distinguishing individual steps from cumulative changes and optimizer
064            weights from stored model weights. We formulate the teacher comparison at a shared
065            student state and examine teacher-conditioned gradients in the appendix.
066
            3. Supervision density (Section 5). We compare sampled-token and top-k supervision in
067
               single-teacher and joint training, and evaluate broader supervision at a common state. Local
068            update concentration and online capability provide complementary measurements of these
069            losses.
070
071   Section 6 brings these studies together in a practical training recipe, connecting supervision choices
072   to per-domain capability retention and specifying the validation still needed.

382   The three studies suggest that capability retention should guide the choice of averaging rule, teacher
383   feedback, and training checkpoint. A useful starting point is to specify the acceptable tradeoff before
384   training. Let sd (θ) be a development-set score for domain d, oriented so that higher is better, and
385
      let ϵd ≥ 0 be a prespecified tolerance relative to the initial student. The proposed recipe selects
      checkpoints subject to
386
387

389                              sd (θ) ≥ sd (θ0 ) − ϵd       for every domain d.                        (7)

391
392
393   Among checkpoints meeting these conditions, the proposed rule selects the one with the highest
394   prespecified aggregate utility, retaining the initial student if none improves it. Selection should
395   account for score uncertainty at the chosen tolerances, followed by evaluation on a separate test set.
396   The constraints govern checkpoint selection rather than add differentiable terms to the distillation
397   loss.
398   With the retention goal fixed, the averaging rule should express the intended importance of each
399   training response. For an objective that weights responses equally within each domain, we use DR
400   with explicit domain weights λd . In our four-domain setting, equal weights and 16 prompts per
401   domain provide the starting allocation. If longer responses are deliberately assigned more weight
402   within a domain, DT expresses that alternative. Under either reduction, the prompt quota and the
403   domain loss weight remain separate choices: with every domain represented and its loss averaged
404   separately, increasing a quota at fixed λd changes exposure and estimation variance without directly
405
      increasing that domain’s expected gradient weight. Generated lengths and truncation rates should
      therefore be recorded alongside both quantities when diagnosing an imbalance.
406
407   Once these weights are explicit, vocabulary feedback can be chosen using its capability profile
408   and computational cost. The joint comparison in Section 5 motivates I64 as a candidate alongside
409   DR, with PG retained as a reference. Its feedback scale also deserves attention. For the student
410   candidate set S and teacher intersection I in Equation 9, the retained student weights sum to r(h) =
411
      pθ (I | h)/pθ (S | h). At a fixed prefix with a nonempty intersection and detached coefficients, the
      I64 gradient is r(h) times the gradient obtained by renormalizing the same weights over I. Thus
412
      removing length weighting does not remove variation in feedback scale across prefixes. Monitoring
413
      this retained mass and empty intersections by domain helps assess whether a fixed candidate size
414   supplies comparable coverage; increasing or renormalizing a small intersection requires validation
415   because it can also amplify unreliable feedback.
416
417
      If a domain repeatedly falls below its retention tolerance, the response should depend on the diag-
      nosed cause. Exposure, length, and coverage checks can reveal inadequate sampling or unintended
418
      weighting. When these do not explain the decline, teacher interactions should be examined at a
419
      common student and optimizer state. The overlap diagnostic defined in Section 4 describes shared
420   update locations; a further functional check is to apply each teacher’s proposed step and measure
421   the change in fixed probe losses from all domains. A zero-current-gradient step provides a reference
422   for the contribution of optimizer history. This comparison can identify local cross-domain harm
423   that coordinate overlap alone does not establish, while subsequent capability evaluations determine
424   whether it persists along training. Gradient modification or task-specific parameters become inter-
425   ventions to test when such harm remains reproducible after the allocation and coverage checks.
426   The default recipe keeps domain weights and prompt quotas fixed; any adaptive variant should state
427   its trigger and adjustment rule separately. To maintain previous-domain exposure without changing
428   the on-policy protocol, retain prompts from those domains and generate fresh responses with the
429   current student before teacher scoring. The resulting starting recipe combines explicit response-
430   level weighting, I64 feedback with coverage monitoring, and retention-based checkpoint selection.
431   Its stability must be assessed both along the training trajectory and across independent runs: an
      acceptable endpoint does not establish that earlier checkpoints avoided degradation.

443
      MOPD combines specialized teachers through feedback on student-generated responses (Ma et al.,
444
      2026). Open-MOPD studies capability imbalance and introduces token-share balancing, gap-based
445   weighting, and reward refresh (Gao et al., 2026). Its token-share correction maps directly to domain-
446   token averaging under the same batch and target domain weights. Our first study separates this
447   between-domain correction from within-domain response weighting and compares their capability
448   outcomes.
449
      Mukherjee et al. (2025) investigate small parameter subsets that support reinforcement-learning fine-
450
      tuning. Yu et al. (2026) find concentrated checkpoint changes under dense distillation supervision
451   and overlap between the changes produced by different teachers in separately trained students. Our
452   second question concerns teachers acting from the same joint student and optimizer state. Our third
453   compares vocabulary supervision through both parameter concentration and capability. Shen et al.
454   (2026) study OPD parameter geometry and select response positions for learning; their position
455   selection concerns a different supervision dimension from the vocabulary alternatives scored at each
456   prefix here.

460
461   The experiments cover one model family and one training seed. The local supervision compari-
462   son uses four prefix banks with high top-k probability coverage, limiting what it establishes about
463
      broader vocabulary tails or other student states. The bootstrap intervals quantify evaluation-question
      uncertainty at fixed checkpoints, not variability across training runs. These limits apply to the pro-
464
      posed recipe as well as to the individual comparisons.
465
466   Local changes in a distillation loss and long-term capability transfer remain distinct measurements.
467   Student-generated responses evolve during training, so a fixed-state diagnostic does not capture the
468   full trajectory. The recipe in Section 6 uses such diagnostics to guide decisions, with stability left to
469
      prospective tests of domain retention under matched budgets and repeated training runs.
