\section{Vocabulary Supervision: Parameter Updates and Task Performance}
\label{sec:density}

Expanding supervision from one sampled token to a larger vocabulary set changes gradient approximation and task-level learning. We compare sampled-token supervision with the student--teacher top-64 intersection during mathematics-only and joint training. Within each setting, initialization, teachers, response averaging, optimizer settings, and prompt schedule are shared. Mathematics-only training uses four times as many mathematics prompts per update as joint training. Full-vocabulary supervision supplies a reference for the gradient and single-step comparisons.

\subsection{Broader supervision produces task-dependent gains}

\paragraph{The mathematics advantage develops during training.}
In mathematics-only training, sampled-token supervision leads top-64 by 2.8 accuracy points at update 100; top-64 leads by 2.2 points at update 500 (Figure~\ref{fig:supervision_overview}c). Joint training also ends with a mathematics gain from top-64 supervision, accompanied by a science loss: final differences are $+2.6$ points on mathematics and $-1.3$ on GPQA. Code and instruction-following differences are smaller.

Top-64's joint-training mean advantage narrows from 1.69 points at update 250 to 0.43 at update 500, with an estimated endpoint 95\% interval of $[-1.01,1.86]$. These trajectories show that the observed benefit depends on the task and the stage of training.

\paragraph{Larger candidate sets redistribute gains across tasks.}
Top-16 and top-64 supervision reach development means of 38.90\% and 39.04\%, and separate-test means of 44.56\% and 44.46\% (Table~\ref{tab:main_outcomes}). On the separate tests, top-64 gains 1.96 mathematics points and loses 2.35 code points relative to top-16.

\subsection{Vocabulary supervision leaves single-step concentration nearly unchanged}

We compare local steps at updates 100 and 500 of the sampled-token and top-64 students, using 96 prefixes in four batches per checkpoint. At fixed parameters, prefixes, and Adam state, sampled-token, top-64, teacher-top-64, and full-vocabulary losses produce nearly identical single-step concentration (Figure~\ref{fig:supervision_overview}a). At update 100 of the sampled-token student, all four change about 88\% of FP32 coordinates and about 0.15\% of BF16 coordinates. This similarity across vocabulary supports is consistent with concentrated stored-weight changes under dense distillation \citep{yu2026denseupdates}.

The teacher-top-64 reference restricts the full-KL gradient sum to the teacher's top-64 tokens using full-vocabulary probabilities. Shared optimizer history contributes strongly to the similar concentration. Even a zero current gradient changes about 0.14\% of BF16 parameters in the same comparison, close to the fraction changed by the supervised losses. Resetting Adam's moments changes the amount of BF16 writeback (Figure~\ref{fig:sampling_history_controls}b,c).
