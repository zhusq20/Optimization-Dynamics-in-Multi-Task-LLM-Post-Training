\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Capability integration and task weighting.}
MOPD consolidates routed domain teachers in policy space
\citep{ma2026mopdmultiteacheronpolicydistillation}.
Open-MOPD diagnoses token-share and reward-related imbalances
\citep{gao2026openmopddiagnosingfixingcapability}.
Our first study separates cross-domain weighting from within-domain
response-length weighting in existing loss reductions.
Prior work on multi-task loss weighting and gradient conflict also
motivates distinguishing weight balance from joint capability gains
\citep{chen2018gradnorm,yu2020gradientsurgerymultitasklearning}.

\paragraph{Sparse parameter changes and teacher differences.}
\citet{mukherjee2025subnetworks} report sparse cumulative parameter changes
in RL and repeated changes in similar parameter subsets across settings.
\citet{yu2026denseupdates} establish related OPD signatures, including
overlap when varying teachers and experiments that restrict training
to selected parameters. \citet{shen2026opdgeometry} examine update
subspaces and response-position thinning. These results motivate our
study but do not establish sparsity of instantaneous gradients.
Teacher compatibility also affects distillation success
\citep{li2026rethinkingopd,ma2026mopdmultiteacheronpolicydistillation}.
We measure single-step and cumulative parameter changes separately and
ask whether teachers with more different distributions change more
different subsets of a shared MOPD student. Raw gradients help explain
the role of the current loss signal.

\paragraph{Vocabulary coverage and gradient estimation.}
MOPD provides sampled-token and corrected teacher-top-$k$ objectives
\citep{ma2026mopdmultiteacheronpolicydistillation}.
Variance-reduced sampled OPD \citep{oh2026vopd}, exact-head plus
sampled-tail estimation \citep{zhang2026emapg}, and tail-aware losses
\citep{huang2026tailaware} motivate separating vocabulary coverage from
the choice of objective and estimator. Our loss study compares
update concentration under practical PG and top-$k$ training, with
full-vocabulary local updates and their gradients in both single-teacher
and joint settings. Established estimator controls are reserved for interpreting
a stable difference; we do not propose a new estimator.
