\begin{abstract}
Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) aims to consolidate specialized
capabilities in a single student. We investigate three aspects of this
process: token balancing, parameter update sparsity, and supervision
density. First, we analyze how token-loss averaging and domain-level
reweighting affect capability balance. A finite-batch gradient
decomposition distinguishes cross-domain token-share weighting from
within-domain response-length weighting, providing an explanation to
test against multi-task training outcomes. Second, building on prior
observations of sparse OPD updates, we characterize parameter update
sparsity in single-teacher and multi-teacher settings. We measure the
overlap between subnetworks updated by different teachers in the same
MOPD student and investigate whether larger teacher--teacher distribution
differences correspond to larger subnetwork differences, while also
examining teacher--student compatibility. Third, we study
\emph{vocabulary-level supervision density}: whether each student-generated
prefix receives supervision from a full-vocabulary loss, a top-$k$
subset, or a sampled-token policy-gradient estimator. We compare the
resulting update sparsity in both single-teacher OPD and MOPD, with
capability measurements providing context for the observed parameter
changes.
\end{abstract}
