\section{Token Balancing and Response Length}
\label{sec:normalization}

\subsection{Why can token balancing change MOPD optimization?}

Equal prompt quotas do not imply equal optimization influence in MOPD: domains and teachers elicit responses of different lengths. The averaging rule therefore chooses what is balanced. GT weights every valid supervised token equally and lets long-response domains contribute more; DT fixes each domain's total weight but still favors longer responses within a domain; DR also weights responses equally within each domain. These choices can change which capabilities dominate a batch, even when the prompt mixture is unchanged.

The distinction is visible directly in the gradient. Writing $g_i=\nabla_\theta\ell_i$, the token-weighted gradient within domain $d$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_d}T_i g_i}{\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_d}T_i}
=\bar g_d+\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_d(T,g)}{\bar T_d}.
\label{eq:weighting}
\end{equation}
The covariance term is nonzero whenever response length and gradient are related. DT retains this term; DR removes it. Correcting GT by the target-to-observed domain token share gives DT exactly, as in Open-MOPD \citep[Section~4.1, Eq.~(8)]{gao2026openmopddiagnosingfixingcapability}. We therefore test two linked questions: whether length weighting changes raw gradients and capability balance, and whether clipping and Adam preserve or mask that difference before it reaches the parameters. Appendix~\ref{app:normalization} gives the derivation.

\subsection{Experiment: averaging rules and capability balance}

\paragraph{Comparison.}
Three online I64 branches differ only in GT, DT, or DR averaging. They share the student initialization, four routed teachers, prompt schedule, and optimizer; each update uses 16 prompts per domain. We compare their trajectories through the common update 500. Appendix~\ref{app:setup} gives the full protocol.

\paragraph{Length changes the path more than the endpoint.}
Mathematics and instruction following each receive 25\% of prompts but contribute 44.05\% and 14.98\% of GT's valid tokens over the first 50 batches; GT therefore gives mathematics nearly three times the weight. DT and DR fix both domains at 25\% (Figure~\ref{fig:all_token_shares}). This imbalance changes early trajectories, yet no rule wins consistently across seeds (Figure~\ref{fig:normalization_capability}). At update 500, four-domain means are $38.58\pm0.75$\% (DR), $38.71\pm0.80$\% (DT), and $38.64\pm0.80$\% (GT). The 0.14-point span is below the 0.75--0.80-point seed standard deviations. MATH and IFBench approach their teachers, LiveCodeBench retains a gap, and GPQA starts near its teacher; rule ranks change across tasks and checkpoints. Here token balancing alters when and where capability improves, not the final aggregate.

\paragraph{The optimizer masks part of the gradient difference.}
On six fixed response banks, the mean raw-gradient cosines are 0.680 (DR--DT), 0.483 (DR--GT), and 0.822 (DT--GT); hence equal domain weights do not make DR and DT equivalent. Global clipping leaves every cosine unchanged, even though it acts very differently online: DR is clipped on all 500 updates and retains a median 9.3\% of its gradient, compared with clipping on 46.2\% of DT updates and 15.0\% of GT updates. Applying one common saved Adam state then raises the three FP32 update cosines to 0.960, 0.939, and 0.979. Adam history therefore changes the effective update rather than merely transmitting the current clipped gradient. Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:optimizer_mediation_moments} shows that it also sets the step scale.

\paragraph{Similar Adam steps explain weak endpoint separation.}
With saved-state Adam, the three reductions' mean immediate held-out KL decreases differ by only $2.9\times10^{-5}$, well within bank variation. Same-norm SGD exposes a wider, checkpoint-dependent spread: all three mean changes improve at update 100 but worsen at update 250. Thus a small downstream separation does not imply that the averaging gradients are equivalent. Clipping changes their magnitudes but not their angles, whereas shared Adam history pulls distinct gradients toward similar updates. Appendix~\ref{app:fixed_batch_normalization} gives the intervention details and all raw-gradient comparisons; Figure~\ref{fig:normalization_ci} reports the paired capability contrasts.
