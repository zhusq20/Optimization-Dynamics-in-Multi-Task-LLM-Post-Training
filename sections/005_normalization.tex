\section{Token Balancing and Capability Allocation}
\label{sec:normalization}

\subsection{Domain weights and response weights}

For response $i$, let $T_i$ be its valid-token count and $\ell_i=T_i^{-1}\sum_t\ell_{i,t}$ its mean token loss. Let $\mathcal B_d$ contain the responses from domain $d$, with target weights $\sum_d\lambda_d=1$. The three reductions in Table~\ref{tab:normalization} expose two distinct choices: the weight assigned to a domain, and the weight assigned to responses within that domain.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{Averaging rules on a fixed batch of nonempty responses. We use $\lambda_d=1/4$. GT assigns domains their observed token shares; DT fixes domain weights; DR also gives each response equal weight within its domain.}
\label{tab:normalization}
\centering\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular*}{\linewidth}{@{\extracolsep{\fill}}ll@{}}
\toprule
Reduction & Batch loss \\
\midrule
Global token (GT) & $\sum_i T_i\ell_i/\sum_i T_i$ \\
Domain token (DT) & $\sum_d\lambda_d\,\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_d}T_i\ell_i/\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_d}T_i$ \\
Domain response (DR) & $\sum_d\lambda_d\,|\mathcal B_d|^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_d}\ell_i$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular*}
\end{table}

With equal prompt quotas, longer responses increase a domain's weight under GT. DT removes this between-domain length effect, while retaining length weighting within each domain. Writing $g_i=\nabla_\theta\ell_i$, the exact fixed-batch relation is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_d}T_i g_i}{\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_d}T_i}
=\bar g_d+\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_d(T,g)}{\bar T_d}.
\label{eq:weighting}
\end{equation}
Thus the effect of DT relative to DR depends on the covariance between response length and gradient, even after domain weights are fixed. Appendix~\ref{app:normalization} derives the identity. The online token-share and response-length traces in Appendix~\ref{app:behavior} show the changing denominators to which these rules apply.

\subsection{Early capability tradeoffs}

Figure~\ref{fig:capability} shows every completed evaluation in the aligned campaign. M-PG reaches 72.0\% on mathematics at update 100, up from 68.8\% initially; instruction following rises from 18.33\% to 19.33\%, while GPQA moves from 32.20\% to 31.19\%. The trajectories therefore reflect domain-specific changes rather than a common gain across capabilities. The single-teacher curves provide a mathematics-specialization reference, with four times as many mathematics prompts per update.

The three I64 reductions can be compared at their shared update-50 checkpoint (Table~\ref{tab:normalization50}). GT leads mathematics, while DR leads instruction following. Relative to DR, GT gains 4.6 percentage points on mathematics, with a paired prompt-bootstrap interval of $[1.2,8.2]$ points. DT changes instruction following by $-2.0$ points, with interval $[-4.0,-0.33]$. Figure~\ref{fig:normalization_ci} gives all eight paired contrasts and the uncertainty procedure. These results make the choice of averaging rule a capability-allocation decision: enforcing equal domain mass changes which responses drive learning, and its value depends on the target capability.
