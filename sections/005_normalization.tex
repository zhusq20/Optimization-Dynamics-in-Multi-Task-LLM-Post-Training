\section{Token Balancing and Response Length}
\label{sec:normalization}

\subsection{How does loss averaging change domain weights?}

The response-normalized objective in Equation~\ref{eq:mopd} gives each response equal weight within its domain. Averaging all valid tokens together instead makes response length part of the weighting rule. This is the distinction underlying token-share balancing in Open-MOPD \citep{gao2026openmopddiagnosingfixingcapability}; length-induced domain imbalance depends on the implemented reduction.

For response $i$, let $T_i$ be its valid-token count and $\ell_i=T_i^{-1}\sum_t\ell_{i,t}$ its mean token loss. Let $\mathcal B_d$ contain the responses from domain $d$, with target weights $\sum_d\lambda_d=1$. Table~\ref{tab:normalization} compares the three reductions on the same batch.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{Averaging rules on a fixed batch of nonempty responses. GT assigns domains their observed token shares; DT fixes domain weights; DR also gives responses equal weight within each domain. Experiments use $\lambda_d=1/4$.}
\label{tab:normalization}
\centering\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular*}{\linewidth}{@{\extracolsep{\fill}}ll@{}}
\toprule
Reduction & Batch loss \\
\midrule
Global token (GT) & $\sum_i T_i\ell_i/\sum_i T_i$ \\
Domain token (DT) & $\sum_d\lambda_d\,\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_d}T_i\ell_i/\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_d}T_i$ \\
Domain response (DR) & $\sum_d\lambda_d\,|\mathcal B_d|^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_d}\ell_i$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular*}
\end{table}

With equal prompt quotas, longer responses increase a domain's weight under GT. DT removes this between-domain length effect, while retaining length weighting within each domain. Writing $g_i=\nabla_\theta\ell_i$, the exact fixed-batch relation is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_d}T_i g_i}{\sum_{i\in\mathcal B_d}T_i}
=\bar g_d+\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_d(T,g)}{\bar T_d}.
\label{eq:weighting}
\end{equation}
Thus domain balancing and response-length normalization address different effects. Equal domain weights need not produce equal gradient magnitudes or compatible directions. Appendix~\ref{app:normalization} derives this weighted-average identity.

\subsection{Experiment: averaging rules and capability balance}

\paragraph{Comparison.}
M-I64-GT, M-I64-DT, and M-I64-DR differ only in the averaging rule. They share initialization, teachers, prompt quotas and schedule, optimizer settings, and the 4,096-token response cap. Each branch generates responses from its evolving student. The common update-50 evaluations provide the complete three-way comparison; DR and GT also have complete update-100 evaluations. Shared settings and coverage appear in Appendix~\ref{app:setup}.

\paragraph{Observed weights.}
In the first 50 rollouts of M-I64-GT, mathematics receives 44.05\% of valid tokens and instruction following 14.98\%, averaged over rollout batches, despite both receiving 25\% of prompts. Under GT these are also their mean loss weights; DT and DR assign each domain 25\%. Appendix~\ref{app:behavior} gives the recorded token-share trajectories. These observations establish the between-domain weighting difference; the covariance identity describes the additional within-domain effect without assuming its sign.

\paragraph{Capability balance.}
Figure~\ref{fig:normalization_capability} and Table~\ref{tab:normalization50} show the corresponding capability tradeoffs. At update 50, GT exceeds DR by 4.6 percentage points on mathematics, with a paired prompt-bootstrap interval of $[1.2,8.2]$ points, while DR exceeds both GT and DT by 2.0 points on instruction following. The DT-minus-DR instruction-following interval is $[-4.0,-0.33]$ points. All eight contrasts appear in Figure~\ref{fig:normalization_ci}. The macro-average and worst-domain change summarize the four scores without assuming that equal domain loss weights must be best. The results show that the averaging rule changes capability allocation; the fixed-batch identity alone does not predict which rule will improve every domain.
