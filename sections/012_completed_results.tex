\subsection{Additional test sets for the same four domains}
\label{app:independent_domain_test}

The ten seed-42 joint-training trajectories use the development benchmarks and checkpoint grid in Appendix~\ref{app:setup}. The following analyses evaluate selected checkpoints on separate test questions, measure candidate-set coverage, and check sensitivity to known training--evaluation overlap.

Table~\ref{tab:independent_test} uses additional test sets fixed before inference, with 256 questions per domain: 1,024 questions and 1,792 responses per model. Mathematics and code come from the Skywork-OR1-RL-Data mathematics and TACO-based code pools; instruction following uses Nemotron-RL-instruction\_following; science uses the Nemotron-RL-knowledge-mcqa validation split restricted by a fixed natural-science keyword filter. Questions are the first eligible unique candidates in the fixed source order, selected without model scores. Normalized exact-match and lexical near-duplicate checks remove questions matching the original training bundle or known evaluation pools. These checks do not establish exhaustive semantic decontamination or rule out pretraining exposure.

Scoring uses mathematics answer verification, code unit tests, instruction-following constraints, and science multiple-choice accuracy. Mathematics, code, and instruction following use greedy decoding; science uses four responses per question at temperature 1. The context budget is 32,768 tokens, with thinking disabled. The data distributions and code/instruction-following scorers differ from the development benchmarks, so their scores are reported separately. The four-domain mean weights domains equally. Question-paired bootstrap intervals quantify finite-test uncertainty and do not estimate variation from training seeds.

\subsection{Checkpoint selection before test evaluation}
\label{app:completed_selection}

We apply the three selection rules in Section~\ref{sec:recipe} to the development grid in Appendix~\ref{app:setup}. Checkpoint choices are fixed before test inference and remain unchanged after the test and overlap-sensitivity analyses. Both development-based rules choose update 250 for PG-DT/Adam and update 500 for the other nine trajectories. The separate PG-DT/Adam update-250 test evaluation allows direct comparison with its update-500 endpoint.

\input{experiments/completed_results_20260917/selection_table}

The retention constraint does not change the checkpoint selected by maximum development mean for any trajectory. On the additional test sets, the update-250 PG-DT/Adam checkpoint scores 0.366 percentage points below its update-500 endpoint in four-domain mean. Thus earlier selection by development mean does not improve the separate-test mean in this case. All selected models satisfy the retention and useful-gain conditions when assessed using test point estimates, which is not a simultaneous confidence guarantee. PG-DR/Adam nevertheless has two development checkpoints outside the retention tolerance: GPQA falls from 32.197\% initially to 28.030\% at update 50 and 30.177\% at update 250, before reaching 34.975\% at update 500. Evaluating only the final checkpoint would miss these intermediate regressions.

\subsection{Probability mass retained by the candidate intersection}
\label{app:actual_intersection_coverage}

For each of four student checkpoints (M-PG and M-I64-DR at updates 100 and 500), four cached response batches provide one response per domain and six sampled positions per response: 96 prefixes per checkpoint. Each response is scored by its domain teacher. We measure $p_\theta(I\mid h)$ for the actual intersection $I=S\cap T$ and the retained fraction $r=p_\theta(I\mid h)/p_\theta(S\mid h)$.

\input{experiments/completed_results_20260917/intersection_coverage_table}

The minimum student probability mass in the actual student--teacher top-64 intersection is 96.55\% over the 384 sampled prefixes, and no intersection is empty (Table~\ref{tab:intersection_coverage}). Thus the single-step I64/full-vocabulary comparisons use prefixes where truncation discards little student probability mass. They do not establish the same behavior when the intersection has low probability mass, with other teachers, or during I16 training. The four batches repeat response sampling at each checkpoint; they are not independent training seeds, and responses may differ between checkpoints.

\subsection{Sensitivity to known mathematics overlap}
\label{app:math_overlap_sensitivity}

The fixed Qwen training pool contains five records matching four known MATH-500 problems, including variants in wording or answer format. The affected MATH test identifiers are counting-and-probability problem 430 and intermediate-algebra problems 1849, 2146, and 582. Their presence in the pool does not show whether a run sampled them or whether they improved its score. We remove these four questions from the existing per-question evaluation results, retaining the original 500-question results as the main protocol.

\input{experiments/completed_results_20260917/math_sensitivity_table}

Removing the four questions leaves the four-domain-mean ranking unchanged for all twelve reported checkpoints. MATH-496 names the retained subset, not a new official benchmark. We keep the checkpoint choices fixed. This analysis does not retrain on a filtered pool or provide an exhaustive contamination audit.
