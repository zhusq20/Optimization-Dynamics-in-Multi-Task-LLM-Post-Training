\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Multi-teacher on-policy distillation.}
MOPD trains a shared student on student-generated responses and routes each
response to a domain teacher
\citep{ma2026mopdmultiteacheronpolicydistillation}. PromptSD uses fixed
per-task rollout counts and adjusts the counts for response length and task
difficulty \citep{mayingzi2026onestudentmanyteachers}. Open-MOPD makes a
closely related distinction between a target token budget and the token share
observed in a mixed batch \citep{gao2026openmopddiagnosingfixingcapability}.
Its token-share correction reweights losses inside a batch. Its gap-following
rule changes domain weights during training. GPAS works at the task-batch
level and keeps a user-selected teacher mixture fixed.

\paragraph{Task allocation in multi-task RL.}
Multi-task methods often change task weights to improve weak tasks or balance
gradient scales \citep{chen2018gradnorm,corrado2026drats}. MT-GRPO learns a
task distribution and uses resampling to match the accepted batch to that
distribution \citep{ramesh2026multitaskgrporeliablellm}. GPAS starts from a
fixed target distribution and adapts the proposal used to estimate its
gradient. The target distribution and the proposal serve separate roles.

\paragraph{Importance sampling for SGD.}
Importance sampling allocates more computation to examples with large
gradient signals while preserving a chosen data objective
\citep{alain2016variancereductionsgddistributed,
katharopoulos2018samplescreatedequaldeep}. GPAS applies the same principle to
task-homogeneous MOPD batches. The MOPD setting adds two practical questions:
teacher calls have a task label, and AdamW can rescale task gradients in
different ways.
