\begin{abstract}


Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) consolidates specialized teacher capabilities into a shared student, yet how training design affects capability integration remains poorly understood. We study three core choices in MOPD: loss averaging, parameter-update sparsity, and vocabulary supervision, using 1.7B and 3B LLMs across mathematics, coding, question answering, and instruction following. For loss averaging, global-token, domain-token, and domain-response averaging objectives show a trade-off between mean performance and domain capability retention: global-token achieves the highest mean performance, while domain-response better preserves the domain capability. For sparsity, we find that parameter updates are sparse in BF16 but dense in FP32, and updates induced by different teachers are strongly aligned and overlapping, challenging the view that specialized capabilities occupy disjoint parameter subsets. For vocabulary supervision, different produce similar per-batch gradient sparsity but different task outcomes: top-$k$ supervision outperforms sampled-token supervision on three of four domains in MOPD training, while single-teacher training retains cross-domain trade-offs. \end{abstract}
