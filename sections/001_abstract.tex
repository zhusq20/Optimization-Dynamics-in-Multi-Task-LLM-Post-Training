\begin{abstract}


Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) consolidates specialized teacher capabilities into a shared student, yet how training design affects capability integration remains poorly understood. We study loss averaging, parameter-update sparsity, and vocabulary supervision using 1.7B and 3B LLMs across mathematics, coding, question answering, and instruction following. Global-token, domain-token, and domain-response averaging produce different capability trajectories but converge within 0.14 mean-score points by update 500. Fixed-state interventions explain this weak endpoint separation: clipping preserves distinct gradient directions, whereas shared Adam history makes the proposed updates strongly aligned. Parameter updates are sparse in BF16 but dense in FP32, and teacher-induced updates are strongly aligned and overlapping, challenging the view that specialized capabilities occupy disjoint parameter subsets. Vocabulary objectives produce similar per-batch gradient sparsity but different task outcomes: top-$k$ supervision outperforms sampled-token supervision on three of four domains in joint training, while single-teacher training retains cross-domain trade-offs. We derive a practical MOPD recipe combining explicit weighting, capability-aware vocabulary supervision, and checkpoint selection under per-domain retention constraints.


\end{abstract}
