\begin{abstract}
Multi-teacher on-policy distillation transfers domain specialists into one student. We organize its study around three questions: how loss averaging affects capability balance, how parameter-update sparsity and teacher overlap behave in a joint student, and whether broader vocabulary supervision changes update sparsity. Experiments use a Qwen3-1.7B student and four domain teachers. Global-token, domain-token, and domain-response averaging assign different weights to response lengths and produce different early capability tradeoffs. At update 50, global-token averaging exceeds domain-response averaging by 4.6 percentage points on mathematics but is 2.0 points lower on instruction following. Joint training retains concentrated cumulative changes in stored BF16 parameters, while FP32 master changes are much denser. At the same joint checkpoint, teacher-conditioned gradients partially overlap; their relationship to teacher-distribution distance depends on the input bank. Comparing sampled-token and top-64 intersection supervision, BF16 cumulative activity reaches 3.41\% and 4.84\% at update 100. Local full-vocabulary comparisons yield very similar update sparsity, while online capability comparisons show no consistent advantage across domains and checkpoints. These results address the three questions within the available measurements, with gradient overlap serving as supporting evidence for teacher interaction.
\end{abstract}
