\begin{abstract}

Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) consolidates specialized teacher capabilities into a shared student, yet how training design affects capability integration remains poorly understood. We study loss averaging, parameter changes, and vocabulary supervision with a Qwen3-1.7B student across mathematics, coding, question answering, and instruction following. Under top-64 intersection supervision, global-token, domain-token, and domain-response averaging produce different capability trajectories but similar four-domain means at update 500. An expanded objective--optimizer comparison shows that this pattern is conditional on the training configuration. At matched states, clipping preserves distinct gradient directions, while shared Adam history increases update alignment; subtracting the zero-current-gradient step removes most alignment between routed teacher updates. Broad FP32 changes coexist with sparse BF16-visible changes, showing why update geometry must be interpreted together with optimizer state and numerical precision. Sampled-token, top-16, and top-64 supervision yield task- and checkpoint-dependent capability differences rather than uniform gains from broader feedback. Separate-domain testing supplies an additional capability comparison, while retention-constrained selection chooses the same checkpoints as unconstrained development utility in the ten tested trajectories. These findings motivate explicit weighting, coverage-aware vocabulary supervision, and per-domain evaluation, while delimiting what local geometry and checkpoint-selection results establish about capability integration.

\end{abstract}
