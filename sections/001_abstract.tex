\begin{abstract}

Multi-task LLM post-training uses task weights to specify capability priorities
and task sampling to allocate compute. Conflating the two makes efficiency
claims hard to interpret; global token averaging can also give long-response
tasks unintended weight.

We present Gradient-Preserving Adaptive Sampling (GPAS) for multi-teacher
on-policy distillation and multi-task reinforcement learning. Each update uses
a task-homogeneous batch and gives every response equal weight. GPAS measures
task gradients in a metric induced by the optimizer: it recovers raw-gradient
sampling for SGD, while our LLM instantiation uses AdamW's stored second
moment. GPAS samples high-impact tasks more often and rescales every sampled
gradient by its target weight divided by its sampling probability. The
conditional expected gradient remains equal to the user-specified task mixture.
The online sampler stores one score per task, and Cost-GPAS also uses measured
task time.

In a controlled three-task experiment, GPAS attains a calculated error ratio of
$0.698$ and a Monte Carlo ratio of $0.697$ relative to uniform sampling; the
cost-aware proposal lowers its local compute proxy by $10.7\%$. The completed
experiment verifies the estimator calculation. Frozen-gradient diagnostics and
three-seed four-teacher MOPD runs will test the AdamW score and progress per
token and GPU hour. A smaller three-seed, three-task GRPO study tests transfer
to reward-based updates. The large-model experiments remain pending in this
draft.

\end{abstract}
