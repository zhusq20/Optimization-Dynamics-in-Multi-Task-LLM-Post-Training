\begin{abstract}

Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) consolidates specialized capabilities into a shared student, but how supervision choices translate into parameter changes and capability gains remains unclear. We study loss averaging, update geometry, and vocabulary supervision by combining online capability evaluation with interventions at matched student and optimizer states. First, balancing domain weights leaves a separate choice of response weighting, which changes raw-gradient directions. Shared Adam history makes the resulting local updates more aligned, while online differences depend on the supervision objective and optimizer. Second, broad FP32 parameter changes coexist with sparse BF16-visible changes. Subtracting the zero-current-gradient step removes most alignment between teachers on their respective domain inputs, showing that apparent agreement can reflect shared optimizer history. Third, broader vocabulary feedback produces similar local update concentration in the measured high-coverage regime, but its capability effects vary across domains and checkpoints. Additional-domain testing also shows task-dependent trade-offs, and retention-constrained selection yields the same checkpoints as unconstrained development utility. Together, these findings show why loss weights, optimizer state, and numerical precision must be considered alongside per-domain capability when evaluating MOPD design choices.

\end{abstract}
