\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multi-teacher on-policy distillation is increasingly used in LLM post-training to consolidate specialized capabilities into a single model \citep{xiaomi2026mimov2flash,nvidia2026nemotron3ultra,kimiteam2026kimik3openfrontier,deepseekai2026deepseekv4highlyefficientmilliontoken}. In this paradigm, specialized teachers provide token-level feedback on responses generated by the student itself. The goal is to integrate their expertise while retaining the student's existing capabilities, but these outcomes need to be assessed separately across domains.

Recent work has advanced both the practice and analysis of this approach. \citet{ma2026mopdmultiteacheronpolicydistillation} demonstrate capability integration using policy-gradient and top-$k$ distillation losses. Open-MOPD identifies uneven capability transfer and develops mechanisms for balancing domain token shares, allocating supervision according to student--teacher gaps, and refreshing student-dependent rewards \citep{gao2026openmopddiagnosingfixingcapability}. The Nemotron 3 Ultra report further finds that top-$k$ and full-vocabulary matching do not outperform sampled-token supervision in its preliminary experiments \citep[Section~3.3.5]{nvidia2026nemotron3ultra}. Complementary studies characterize the sparsity and geometry of OPD parameter changes, including their concentration at checkpoint precision and their evolution during training \citep{yu2026denseupdates,shen2026opdgeometry}. These findings motivate examining how supervision choices translate into parameter changes and capability outcomes in a shared student.

Several distinctions are central to this examination. Balancing domain weights still leaves open how responses of different lengths are weighted within each domain. Concentrated changes in stored model weights do not, by themselves, describe the breadth of optimizer updates. Likewise, supervising more vocabulary alternatives need not produce a proportional increase in changed parameters, and similar local update concentration may accompany different capability outcomes. We therefore ask: \emph{how do loss averaging and vocabulary supervision affect learning in MOPD, and what do parameter-change measurements reveal about these choices?}

We combine algebraic analysis of loss averaging, local interventions at fixed student and optimizer states, and capability evaluations along training. The interventions isolate how a design choice changes a local update; online comparisons measure the capability outcomes as each student's responses and optimizer history evolve. Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Separating domain balancing from response weighting (Section~\ref{sec:normalization}).}
We distinguish global-token, domain-token, and domain-response averaging and show that within-domain length weighting changes raw-gradient direction even with identical domain weights. Clipping preserves these directions, whereas shared Adam history makes the local updates substantially more similar. Similar final mean scores under top-64 supervision coexist with different capability trajectories; the expanded objective--optimizer comparison limits how broadly this endpoint pattern can be generalized.
\item \textbf{Separating current teacher signals from history and precision effects (Section~\ref{sec:subnetworks}).}
We show that broad FP32 changes coexist with sparse BF16-visible changes, and that teacher-update alignment is sensitive to shared optimizer history. Subtracting the step produced with zero current gradient removes most alignment under domain-routed inputs. Update overlap and direction therefore need to be interpreted with both numerical representation and optimizer state specified.
\item \textbf{Comparing vocabulary supervision through both updates and capability (Section~\ref{sec:density}).}
Sampled-token, top-$k$, and full-vocabulary losses produce similar local update concentration in the measured high-coverage regime. Online sampled-token, top-16, and top-64 comparisons nevertheless show domain- and checkpoint-dependent trade-offs, with no uniform gain from broader feedback. Local concentration alone is therefore insufficient to rank supervision choices by capability.
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:recipe} draws practical implications for weighting, update diagnostics, and checkpoint selection. An additional-domain test provides a separate capability comparison; retention-constrained and unconstrained utility selection choose the same checkpoints in the tested trajectories, so these results do not establish an added benefit from the constraint.
