\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multi-teacher on-policy distillation is increasingly used in LLM post-training to consolidate specialized capabilities into a single model. Recent examples include MiMo-V2-Flash \citep{xiaomi2026mimov2flash}, Nemotron 3 Ultra \citep{nvidia2026nemotron3ultra}, Kimi K3 \citep{kimiteam2026kimik3openfrontier}, and DeepSeek-V4 \citep{deepseekai2026deepseekv4highlyefficientmilliontoken}. In this paradigm, specialized teachers provide token-level feedback on responses generated by the student itself, allowing independently developed experts to transfer their capabilities to a shared student while preserving the student's own ability.

Recent work has advanced both the practice and analysis of this approach. \citet{ma2026mopdmultiteacheronpolicydistillation} demonstrate capability integration using policy-gradient and top-$k$ distillation losses. Open-MOPD identifies uneven capability transfer and develops mechanisms for balancing domain token shares, allocating supervision according to student--teacher gaps, and refreshing student-dependent rewards \citep{gao2026openmopddiagnosingfixingcapability}. The Nemotron 3 Ultra report further finds that top-$k$ and full-vocabulary matching do not outperform sampled-token supervision in its preliminary experiments \citep[Section~3.3.5]{nvidia2026nemotron3ultra}. Complementary studies characterize the sparsity and geometry of OPD parameter changes, including their concentration at checkpoint precision and their evolution during training \citep{yu2026denseupdates,shen2026opdgeometry}. These findings motivate examining how supervision choices translate into parameter changes and capability outcomes in a shared student.

Several distinctions are central to this examination. Balancing domain weights still leaves open how responses of different lengths are weighted within each domain. Concentrated changes in stored model weights do not, by themselves, describe the breadth of optimizer updates. Likewise, supervising more vocabulary alternatives need not produce a proportional increase in changed parameters, and similar local update concentration may accompany different capability outcomes. We therefore ask: \emph{how do loss averaging and vocabulary supervision affect learning in MOPD, and what do parameter-change measurements reveal about these choices?}

We investigate this question through controlled comparisons with a Qwen3-1.7B student and four domain teachers covering mathematics, code, instruction following, and science. We combine algebraic analysis of loss averaging, local measurements at fixed student and optimizer states, and capability evaluations along training. Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Separating domain balancing from response weighting (Section~\ref{sec:normalization}).}
We distinguish global-token, domain-token, and domain-response averaging and show that within-domain length weighting changes gradient direction even when domain weights are identical. At the common update-50 comparison, global-token averaging achieves the highest mean score, while domain-response averaging yields the smallest worst-domain decline from initialization. The choice therefore depends on the intended capability tradeoff.
\item \textbf{Characterizing parameter changes across numerical representations (Section~\ref{sec:subnetworks}).}
We measure individual optimizer steps and cumulative changes separately in FP32 optimizer weights and BF16 model weights. At update 250, cumulative changes in the two joint-training branches are nonzero in 5.9--7.1\% of BF16 coordinates and 95.7--96.1\% of FP32 coordinates. These measurements clarify how sparse stored changes coexist with broad optimization activity in MOPD.
\item \textbf{Comparing vocabulary supervision through both updates and capability (Section~\ref{sec:density}).}
At the tested shared state, sampled-token, top-$k$, and full-vocabulary losses produce similar local update concentration. Online training nevertheless yields different capability profiles: in four-teacher training at update 250, student--teacher top-64 intersection (I64) supervision scores higher than sampled-token supervision in three domains and ties on instruction following. The mathematics-only teacher setting retains cross-domain tradeoffs, motivating evaluation beyond local sparsity alone.
\end{enumerate}

We use these findings to outline a candidate MOPD recipe combining explicit supervision weights, vocabulary feedback selected using capability evaluations, and checkpoint selection under per-domain retention constraints (Section~\ref{sec:recipe}). Its combined effectiveness and stability remain to be validated.
