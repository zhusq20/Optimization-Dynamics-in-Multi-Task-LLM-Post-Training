\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================================

Multi-task post-training needs separate controls for the target objective and
compute allocation. Response-normalized task batches define the unit of the
target; inverse sampling preserves its conditional mean. GPAS chooses proposals
in an optimizer-induced metric, and Cost-GPAS also uses measured task time. The
identity metric recovers the SGD gradient-norm rule; our LLM experiments focus
on the lagged AdamW metric.

The controlled experiment supports the local estimator claim. GPAS reduces the
measured error by $30.3\%$, and Cost-GPAS reduces the time-weighted proxy by
$10.7\%$. The constructed comparison shows that coordinate scale can matter.
The planned gradient-bank and exact-step audits will measure whether the lagged
AdamW score remains useful in LLM training.

Three boundaries guide interpretation. First, the correction preserves the
conditional mean of the gradient delivered to the optimizer. Stateful
optimizers can still produce different parameter paths under different task
orders.
Second, the score combines mean scale and batch variation. Compatibility and
gradient-bank audits measure whether a large score carries a consistent
training signal. Third, Cost-GPAS optimizes an uncentered local compute score.
The GPU-hour curves provide the end-to-end efficiency measurement.

The present draft contains controlled estimator evidence and a fixed
large-model protocol. Its corrected KL-trajectory baseline compares a recent
curriculum signal with GPAS while holding the target mixture fixed. Completion
of E1--E4 is required for a claim about MOPD training efficiency, and E5 is
required for a GRPO transfer claim. The evidence boundary keeps the mathematical
result, the MOPD hypothesis, and the eventual capability results distinct.
