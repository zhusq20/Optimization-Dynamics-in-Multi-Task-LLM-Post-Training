\section{Discussion}

The three studies connect loss averaging, teacher interaction, and vocabulary supervision to multi-teacher distillation. First, domain-token averaging fixes between-domain weights, while domain-response averaging also removes within-domain length weighting; their capability tradeoffs differ in the observed online runs. Second, joint training retains concentrated cumulative changes in stored BF16 parameters. Teacher-conditioned gradients partially overlap at one joint checkpoint, and their relationship to teacher distribution distance depends on the inputs. Third, PG and I64 both yield concentrated BF16 changes, with broader cumulative activity for I64 at update 100 but little difference in the measured local sparsity statistics. Capability results do not consistently favor either loss.

The evidence covers one model family, four related teachers, and one training seed. Joint online measurements reach update 100, and the local comparisons use two small, high-coverage prefix banks at that checkpoint. Single-teacher online parameter geometry, teacher-specific optimizer-step overlap, and its relationship to teacher distance remain open parts of the original questions. Completing these comparisons and the common capability checkpoints would strengthen the three studies. Optimizer-state and input controls serve to interpret the reported measurements; they do not replace those comparisons.
