\section{Update Sparsity and Teacher Overlap}
\label{sec:subnetworks}

We examine update sparsity first, then parameter overlap between teachers in the same student, and finally its relationship to teacher-distribution differences.

\subsection{Does update sparsity persist under multiple teachers?}

For cumulative changes $\Delta=\theta-\theta_0$ and individual optimizer steps $u=\theta_{\mathrm{after}}-\theta_{\mathrm{before}}$, we measure the active fraction $f_\tau(z)=P^{-1}\sum_j\mathbf 1\{|z_j|>\tau\}$ and the squared norm carried by the largest-magnitude coordinates. We also report the smallest coordinate fraction containing 90\% of squared change. Thresholds and energy concentration distinguish small overall movement from concentration on a few parameters. FP32 master changes and stored BF16 changes are measured separately over 1,720,574,976 unique parameters.

\paragraph{Experiment and observations.}
The matched M-PG and M-I64-DR branches provide online measurements at updates 1, 50, and 100 (Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}). At update 100, M-PG has changed 3.41\% of BF16 coordinates, with 90\% of squared change concentrated in 1.82\% of coordinates. M-I64-DR has changed 4.84\%, with 90\% of squared change in 2.48\%. Thus concentrated stored-parameter changes are present in the measured joint runs, including the broader I64 supervision.

The FP32 cumulative changes are much denser: their nonzero fractions are 94.00\% for PG and 94.45\% for I64, and their 90\%-energy fractions are 34.46\% and 35.84\%. The actual M-PG FP32 optimizer step at update 100 has 88.13\% nonzero coordinates. Sparsity of the exported model therefore does not imply equally sparse optimizer movement. Appendix~\ref{app:records} records the thresholds and the saved-state optimizer control used to interpret this distinction.

The single-teacher runs provide the capability reference in Section~\ref{sec:density}, but their corresponding online parameter measurements are not available here. The present result establishes concentration in MOPD; it does not yet determine whether multiple teachers increase or decrease sparsity relative to single-teacher OPD.

\subsection{Do teachers affect the same parameters in one student?}

The comparison of interest applies each teacher's loss from identical copies of the same MOPD student and optimizer state. For a proposed step $u_d$, select the largest-magnitude $\rho$ fraction of coordinates, denoted $S_d(\rho)$, and measure
\begin{equation}
J(d,e)=\frac{|S_d(\rho)\cap S_e(\rho)|}{|S_d(\rho)\cup S_e(\rho)|}.
\label{subnet_overlap}
\end{equation}
This compares selected locations at the same selection size. Shared locations can receive compatible or opposing updates, so overlap has no predetermined good or bad direction.

\paragraph{Available teacher-conditioned evidence.}
At M-PG update 100, the completed teacher-pair measurements apply Equation~\ref{subnet_overlap} to raw gradients, selecting $\rho=1\%$. Each of two banks contains one student response per domain and four selected prefixes per response. In the routed comparison, every teacher scores its own domain response. Figure~\ref{fig:teacher_overlap} shows partial overlap for every pair: mean Jaccard is 0.0951 for PG and 0.0999 for I64 across the two banks and six pairs.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/aligned_evidence_20260910/teacher_overlap.pdf}
\caption{Teacher-pair overlap at the same M-PG update-100 checkpoint. Cells show mean Jaccard over two banks for the largest-magnitude 1\% of routed gradient coordinates. The two panels use the same scale. These are gradient selections, not measured teacher-specific optimizer-step supports.}
\label{fig:teacher_overlap}

When every teacher instead scores the same four-response bank with equal domain weights, mean gradient overlap rises to 0.3808 for PG and 0.4577 for I64. This control changes both input composition and response count. A comparison fixing the prefix count is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:records}, together with gradient norms and signed alignment. These observations identify shared gradient locations within one student. Teacher-specific optimizer-step overlaps are not recorded in this snapshot, so the gradient sets cannot establish isolated teacher subnetworks or allocate past joint parameter changes to teachers.

\subsection{Do more different teachers affect more different parameters?}

\paragraph{Experiment.}
Every teacher scores the same cached student prefixes. We compute full-vocabulary Jensen--Shannon (JS) divergence for each teacher pair, averaging over the same domains and prefixes, and pair it with the parameter-selection distance $1-J(d,e)$. The available comparison again uses gradient selections at M-PG update 100. Appendix~\ref{app:teacher_distance} gives the distribution-distance formula.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/aligned_evidence_20260910/teacher_js.pdf}
\caption{Teacher distribution difference versus gradient-selection difference. Colors identify the six teacher pairs; marker shapes identify the two banks. Each panel uses the same teacher JS measurements, paired with PG or I64 gradient overlap on routed or common inputs.}
\label{fig:teacher_js}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Observation.}
Figure~\ref{fig:teacher_js} shows that routed selection distances occupy a narrow band, whereas common-input distances vary more widely. The relationship depends on the input bank and supervision choice; these measurements do not establish a general rule that more different teacher distributions update more different parameters. The six pairs share teachers, and both banks use one student checkpoint. The result is a description of this teacher pool, with the proposed-step version of the question still open.
