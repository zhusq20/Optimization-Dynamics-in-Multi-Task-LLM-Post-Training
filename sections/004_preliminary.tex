\section{A Fixed Objective under Task Sampling}
\label{sec:preliminaries}
% ============================================================================

There are $M$ tasks. Task $i$ has loss $L_i$ and objective weight
$\lambda_i\geq 0$, with $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$. These weights define
Equation~\ref{eq:fixed_multitask_objective}. Each step draws one task
$I\sim q$, forms a task-homogeneous batch, and computes a task gradient $G_I$.
All expectations in this section condition on the model, optimizer state, and
sampling information available before the draw.

Our MOPD batches use a fixed valid-token budget. Tokens are averaged inside a
response, followed by an average over responses. This gives one consistent
definition of $G_i$ even when response lengths differ. The analysis also
applies to other within-task aggregation rules once they are fixed in advance.

For every task with positive objective weight, we require $q_i>0$ and set
\begin{equation}
    w_i=\frac{\lambda_i}{q_i},
    \qquad \widehat g=w_I G_I.
    \label{eq:importance_estimator}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Standard importance correction]
\label{lem:importance_correction}
The corrected gradient has mean
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb E[\widehat g]
    =\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbb E[G_i]
    =\nabla F(\theta).
    \label{eq:importance_unbiased}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

This familiar importance-sampling result fixes the weighted gradient target
for any proposal. A curriculum may change $\lambda$ between steps; the same
statement applies after each change.

The correction interacts with AdamW through its second moment. We use
$w_I G_I$ for the first-moment observation and $w_I G_I^2$ for the
second-moment observation:
\begin{align}
    m^+&=\beta_1m+(1-\beta_1)w_I G_I,\notag\\
    v^+&=\beta_2v+(1-\beta_2)w_I G_I^2,
    \label{eq:moment_preserving_adamw}
\end{align}
where the square is element-wise. Bias correction, weight decay, and the
parameter update stay unchanged. We call this rule moment-consistent AdamW.

\begin{proposition}[Proposal-independent second-moment target]
\label{prop:conditional_preservation}
At a fixed training state, let $S_i=\mathbb E[G_i^2]$. The mean observation in
the moment-consistent second-moment update is
\begin{equation}
    \sum_i\lambda_i S_i,
    \label{eq:consistent_second_moment_target}
\end{equation}
which is independent of $q$. Standard AdamW observes $(w_I G_I)^2$ and has
mean
\begin{equation}
    \sum_i\frac{\lambda_i^2}{q_i}S_i.
    \label{eq:standard_adam_second_moment}
\end{equation}
If the task-gradient distributions and weights were held fixed, these two
means would be the respective population targets of the exponential average.
\end{proposition}

Appendix~\ref{app:moment_target_proof} gives the two-line calculation. The
claim concerns the second-moment target at a fixed state. Task order can still
produce different parameter paths, and the target moves as the model learns.

The short-memory limit gives useful intuition. Ignore momentum, weight decay,
and $\epsilon$, and let the current observation dominate $v$. For a positive
importance multiplier, the two scaled updates become
\begin{equation}
  \frac{wG}{\sqrt{w^2G^2}}=\operatorname{sign}(G),
  \qquad
  \frac{wG}{\sqrt{wG^2}}=\sqrt{w}\,\operatorname{sign}(G).
  \label{eq:two_adam_limits}
\end{equation}
Standard AdamW removes the multiplier in this idealized limit, while the
moment-consistent rule retains its square root. With long second-moment memory,
the moment-consistent denominator tracks
Equation~\ref{eq:consistent_second_moment_target}; the standard denominator
moves with $q$ through Equation~\ref{eq:standard_adam_second_moment}. This
sign-and-scale view follows the Adam interpretation of
\citet{balles2018dissecting}.
