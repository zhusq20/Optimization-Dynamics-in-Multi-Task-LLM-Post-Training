\section{Vocabulary Supervision: Parameter Updates and Task Performance}
\label{sec:density}

Supervising more vocabulary tokens per prefix does not necessarily make parameter updates less concentrated. Consistent with the concentrated checkpoint changes observed under dense distillation losses \citep{yu2026denseupdates}, we find nearly identical one-step update concentration for sampled-token, top-$k$, and full-vocabulary losses when applied to the same student and Adam state. End-to-end training nevertheless produces different task scores: I64 ends with higher mathematics accuracy and lower GPQA accuracy than PG. We examine both measurements to distinguish immediate optimizer behavior from the performance of the trained student.

\subsection{Vocabulary support and experimental controls}

PG supervises one sampled token at each prefix; I64 supervises the intersection of the student's and teacher's top-64 token sets (Equations~\ref{eq:pg} and~\ref{eq:topk}). We compare S-PG and S-I64 trained with the mathematics teacher, and M-PG and M-I64-DR trained jointly with four domain teachers. Within each setting, the runs use the same initialization, teachers, domain-response averaging, optimizer settings, and prompt schedule. The single-teacher runs use 64 mathematics responses per update; joint runs use 16 per domain. We compare PG and I64 after the same number of optimizer updates. Joint I16 training uses the same intersection objective and averaging rule, with $k=16$. These comparisons use seed 42.

The one-step comparison evaluates PG, I64, teacher-top-64 supervision (T64), and full-vocabulary reverse KL at four joint-training checkpoints: PG and I64-DR after 100 and 500 updates. T64 uses the teacher's top-64 set without requiring membership in the student's top-64 set, with the gradient correction defined in Appendix~\ref{app:losses}. At each checkpoint, all four losses use the same 96 sampled prefixes, grouped into four batches, with identical prefix weights and saved Adam moments. We measure the fraction of changed parameters and the fraction of squared step norm carried by the largest 1\% of coordinates.

\subsection{Update concentration}

\paragraph{More supervised tokens leave one-step concentration nearly unchanged.}
At M-PG/100, each of the four losses produces an optimizer step that changes about 88.0\% of FP32 master weights (Table~\ref{tab:local_concentration}). After conversion to BF16, only 0.149\% of parameters change for PG and 0.150\% for I64, T64, and full vocabulary. The largest 1\% of coordinates account for about 8.8\% of the FP32 squared step norm and all of the BF16 squared step norm. BF16 nonzero fractions are also similar between losses at the other three checkpoints (Figure~\ref{fig:supervision_overview}a). Adam's accumulated moments contribute to these changes: even with a zero current gradient, its step changes 0.140\% of BF16 parameters at M-PG/100.

At each of the four checkpoints, the actual I64 intersection retains more than 99.9\% of student probability mass on average; the minimum over all 384 prefixes is 96.55\%. Thus I64 already includes nearly all student probability mass in this comparison, despite supervising at most 64 vocabulary tokens. These measurements do not determine what happens when top-$k$ sets omit substantial probability mass. Table~\ref{tab:intersection_coverage} reports the intersection size, probability mass, and retained weight ratio by checkpoint; Appendix~\ref{app:records} provides threshold and optimizer-state controls.

The raw gradients still differ even when the resulting optimizer steps have similar concentration. In a separate experiment with fixed prefixes, averaging 1, 16, and 64 PG token samples raises mean gradient cosine with the full-vocabulary gradient from 0.537 to 0.865 to 0.967 (Figure~\ref{fig:sampling_history_controls}a). Finite token sampling therefore explains part of the gradient difference between PG and full-vocabulary supervision. Separate zero-gradient and moment-reset controls quantify how the saved Adam moments affect the number of BF16 weights that change (panels b,c).

\paragraph{Similar single-step concentration does not imply similar cumulative changes.}
After 500 joint-training updates, 8.46\% of I64's BF16 weights differ from initialization, compared with 7.30\% for PG (Section~\ref{sec:subnetworks}; Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}). The one-step experiment fixes the student, prefixes, and Adam moments. During end-to-end training, each loss instead changes the student, its subsequent sampled responses, and the accumulated optimizer moments. The similarity of one-step concentration therefore does not extend to the number of parameters changed over training.

\subsection{Capability under single-teacher and joint training}

\paragraph{I64's mathematics advantage emerges late and does not extend to every task.}
In single-teacher training, PG leads I64 on mathematics by 2.8 percentage points after 100 updates, but I64 leads PG by 2.2 points after 500 updates (Figure~\ref{fig:supervision_overview}c). At update 500, I64 also scores 0.67 points higher on instruction following, but 1.56 points lower on code and 1.77 points lower on GPQA. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:all_capability} gives the scores, and Table~\ref{tab:endpoint_paired} gives the paired uncertainty intervals.

Joint training shows the same endpoint trade-off between mathematics and GPQA: I64-minus-PG differences are $+2.60$ points on mathematics, $0.00$ on code, $+0.33$ on instruction following, and $-1.77$ on GPQA (Table~\ref{tab:completed_endpoints}). All four nominal paired 95\% intervals include zero, so these point estimates do not establish a consistent task-level improvement (Table~\ref{tab:endpoint_paired}). The ranking also changes during training. At update 250, I64 scores higher on mathematics, code, and GPQA and ties PG on instruction following; its four-domain mean advantage then shrinks from 1.69 to 0.29 points by update 500.

\paragraph{Top-64 supervision does not consistently outperform top-16.}
Joint I16 and I64 have endpoint four-domain means of 38.65\% and 38.58\%, respectively (Table~\ref{tab:completed_endpoints}). On the separate test sets, their means are again close, at 44.56\% and 44.46\%, but I64 scores 1.96 points higher on mathematics and 1.17 points higher on science, while I16 scores 2.35 points higher on code and 1.17 points higher on instruction following (Table~\ref{tab:independent_test}). These estimates establish neither statistical equivalence nor a general advantage for either candidate-set size. Changing $k$ changes both the supervised token set and the normalization of token weights in Equation~\ref{eq:topk}; it does not isolate token count alone. I16 is evaluated in end-to-end training, without a corresponding one-step geometry measurement.
