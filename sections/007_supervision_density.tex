\section{Vocabulary Supervision: Updates and Capability}
\label{sec:density}

Broader vocabulary supervision can change the gradient at a prefix without proportionally increasing the number of parameters changed by the optimizer. Dense supervision and concentrated checkpoint changes coexist in the experiments of \citet{yu2026denseupdates}. We examine this relationship through a local comparison at a common state, online PG/I64 comparisons in single-teacher and joint training, and a joint I16 branch that varies the intersection support size.

\subsection{Comparison and measurements}

PG and I64 use Equations~\ref{eq:pg} and~\ref{eq:topk}. The online configurations are S-PG and S-I64 with the mathematics teacher, and M-PG and M-I64-DR with four routed teachers. Within each setting, the branches share initialization, teachers, domain-response averaging, optimizer settings, and prompt schedule. The single-teacher branches use 64 mathematics responses per update; joint branches use 16 per domain. We compare PG and I64 within each setting at common checkpoints. The added joint I16 branch uses the same intersection definition and DR averaging, with $k=16$ rather than 64. All added comparisons use seed 42.

The local experiment evaluates PG, I64, corrected teacher-top-64 supervision (T64), and full-vocabulary reverse KL at four joint student states: PG and I64-DR at updates 100 and 500. Within each state, all losses use identical prefix weights and the same saved optimizer state, with four banks totaling 96 sampled prefixes. We measure the fraction of changed parameters and the squared step norm carried by the largest 1\% of coordinates. Appendix~\ref{app:losses} gives the bank construction and T64 definition.

\subsection{Update concentration}

\paragraph{Local changes are similarly concentrated across losses.}
At M-PG/100, all four losses change about 88.0\% of FP32 coordinates (Table~\ref{tab:local_concentration}). Mean BF16 nonzero fractions are 0.149\% for PG and 0.150\% for I64, T64, and full vocabulary. The largest 1\% of coordinates carry about 8.8\% of the FP32 squared step norm and all of the BF16 squared step norm. Local concentration is therefore very similar across these losses, as are the BF16 nonzero fractions at the other measured states (Figure~\ref{fig:supervision_overview}a). Shared history contributes to this result: a zero-current-gradient control alone changes 0.140\% of BF16 coordinates at M-PG/100.

Across these four states, the actual teacher--student intersection contains more than 99.9\% of student probability on average, and none of the 384 sampled prefixes falls below 95\%. The local comparison therefore describes a high-coverage regime. Table~\ref{tab:intersection_coverage} gives the mass, support size, and retained weight ratio by state; Appendix~\ref{app:records} provides threshold and optimizer-state controls.

In a separate fixed-prefix sampling control, increasing PG draws from 1 to 16 to 64 raises mean gradient cosine with full vocabulary from 0.537 to 0.865 to 0.967 (Figure~\ref{fig:sampling_history_controls}a). This supports sampling variability as one source of their local difference. Separate zero-gradient and moment-reset controls show how optimizer history affects BF16-visible changes (panels b,c).

\paragraph{Joint trajectories accumulate different changes.}
Despite similar local concentration, joint I64 training accumulates broader BF16-visible changes than PG (Section~\ref{sec:subnetworks}; Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}). The local experiment fixes the student, prefixes, and optimizer state; the online comparison includes the feedback between each loss and the responses its student generates. Similar local concentration therefore does not imply identical training trajectories.

\subsection{Capability under single-teacher and joint training}

Figure~\ref{fig:supervision_overview}(c) summarizes task-wise differences from PG. In single-teacher training, PG's mathematics score exceeds I64 by 2.8 points at update 100, whereas I64 exceeds PG by 2.2 points at update 500. At the endpoint, I64 also scores higher on instruction following, while PG scores higher on code and GPQA. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:all_capability} gives the single-teacher and intermediate-checkpoint scores.

At the joint-training endpoint, I64-minus-PG differences are $+2.60$ points on mathematics, $0.00$ on code, $+0.33$ on instruction following, and $-1.77$ on GPQA (Table~\ref{tab:completed_endpoints}). All four nominal paired 95\% intervals include zero (Table~\ref{tab:endpoint_paired}). Earlier, at update 250, I64 scores higher in three domains and ties on instruction following; its mean advantage shrinks from 1.69 to 0.29 points by update 500. The apparent advantage therefore depends on the domain and checkpoint.

\paragraph{Increasing the intersection size does not yield uniform gains.}
Joint I16 and I64 have endpoint means of 38.65\% and 38.58\%, respectively (Table~\ref{tab:completed_endpoints}). Their additional-domain test means are 44.56\% and 44.46\%, with different per-domain trade-offs (Table~\ref{tab:independent_test}). These point estimates do not establish equivalence or a general advantage for either support size. Changing $k$ changes both the candidate set and its normalization; it is not a pure intervention on supervision density. I16 is an online capability comparison and is not included in the local geometry panels.

Broader feedback yields nearly unchanged concentration at matched states in the measured regime, while the trained students develop different capability profiles. Both measurements are needed to assess supervision choices.
