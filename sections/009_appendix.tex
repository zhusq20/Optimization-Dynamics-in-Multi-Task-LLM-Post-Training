\section{Derivations and Evaluation Details}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Moment targets}
\label{app:moment_target_proof}

For Lemma~\ref{lem:importance_correction}, conditioning on the state before the
draw gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[w_I G_I]
=\sum_i q_i\frac{\lambda_i}{q_i}\mathbb E[G_i]
=\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbb E[G_i].
\end{equation}
The same calculation with an element-wise square gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[w_I G_I^2]=\sum_i\lambda_i S_i.
\end{equation}
Standard AdamW squares the complete corrected gradient, so
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[w_I^2 G_I^2]
=\sum_i\frac{\lambda_i^2}{q_i}S_i.
\end{equation}
These are the observations entering the exponential average in
Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional_preservation}.

\subsection{GPAS derivation and the descent connection}
\label{app:gpas_proof}

Let $A$ be the fixed diagonal scale with entries
$1/(\sqrt{\bar v}+\epsilon)$, let
$h_i=\mathbb E[G_i]$, and let $h=\sum_i\lambda_i h_i$. The centered scaled
error of one corrected draw is
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E\|A(\widehat g-h)\|_2^2
=\sum_i\frac{\lambda_i^2s_i^2}{q_i}-\|Ah\|_2^2.
\label{eq:centered_optimizer_mse}
\end{equation}
The final term is fixed before sampling. Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{equation}
\sum_i\frac{\lambda_i^2s_i^2}{q_i}
\geq \left(\sum_i\lambda_i s_i\right)^2,
\end{equation}
with equality when $q_i$ is proportional to $\lambda_i s_i$.

For the local optimization connection, consider the preconditioned update
$\theta^+=\theta-\eta A\widehat g$ and an $L$-smooth objective. The descent
lemma and unbiasedness give
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[F(\theta^+)]
\leq F(\theta)-\eta\langle h,Ah\rangle
+\frac{L\eta^2}{2}
\sum_i\frac{\lambda_i^2s_i^2}{q_i}.
\label{eq:descent_connection}
\end{equation}
At a fixed state and learning rate, the last term contains all dependence on
the proposal. AdamW momentum, changing preconditioners, and weight decay make
this a local comparison rather than a trajectory theorem.

\subsection{Cost connection}
\label{app:cost_connection}

Write $\bar c(q)=\sum_iq_ic_i$. A time budget $B$ gives approximately
$T=B/\bar c(q)$ steps. A standard smooth non-convex stochastic bound has the
form
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb E\|\nabla F(\theta_t)\|^2
\leq \frac{C_1}{\eta T}+C_2\eta V(q),
\end{equation}
for constants independent of $q$ under a fixed, bounded preconditioner. Tuning
$\eta$ in this bound leaves a $q$-dependent factor proportional to
$\sqrt{\bar c(q)V(q)/B}$. This motivates the product $J(q)$ as a
compute-efficiency proxy.

A second Cauchy--Schwarz calculation gives
\begin{equation}
J(q)=\left(\sum_iq_ic_i\right)
\left(\sum_i\frac{\lambda_i^2s_i^2}{q_i}\right)
\geq\left(\sum_i\lambda_i s_i\sqrt{c_i}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
Equality holds at
$q_i\propto\lambda_i s_i/\sqrt{c_i}$.

\subsection{Sampled-token MOPD gradient}
\label{app:opd_estimators}

Fix a student-generated prefix $z$. Write
$p_v=\pi_\theta(v\mid z)$ and $\tau_v=T_i(v\mid z)$. The reverse-KL loss
gradient at that prefix is
\begin{equation}
    g_i(z)=\sum_v p_v\log\frac{p_v}{\tau_v}
    \nabla_\theta\log p_v.
    \label{eq:full_token_rkl_update}
\end{equation}
For $V\sim p$, the sampled-token gradient
\begin{equation}
    G_i(z,V)=
    \operatorname{sg}\!\left[\log\frac{p_V}{\tau_V}\right]
    \nabla_\theta\log p_V
    \label{eq:pg_prefix_unbiased}
\end{equation}
has conditional mean $g_i(z)$. The implementation averages these token
gradients inside each response and then averages responses to form $G_i$.

\subsection{Frozen-gradient evaluation}
\label{app:frozen_metrics}

At one checkpoint, the gradient bank estimates each task mean $h_i$, raw
second moment, preconditioned second moment $s_i^2$, and runtime $c_i$.
Proposal scales are estimated on one fold and evaluated on the other. Reversing
the folds and averaging uses every batch while preserving held-out evaluation.
The primary local quantities are $V(q)$ and $J(q)$ because they avoid the
upward bias of a sample mean norm in centered MSE. A split estimate of
Equation~\ref{eq:centered_optimizer_mse} is reported as a secondary check.

To evaluate update prediction, each held-out $G_i$ is inserted into the saved
AdamW state while every other input is held fixed. The task-dependent update is
$\Delta\theta(G_i)-\Delta\theta(0)$, which removes weight decay and state-only
terms. Pearson and Spearman correlations are computed within tasks and on the
pooled bank. Pairwise cosine similarity uses task mean gradients under one
shared random projection.

For state replay, a task sequence is drawn from each proposal. Standard AdamW
accumulates $w^2G_i^2$, and moment-consistent AdamW accumulates $wG_i^2$.
Population-target error is computed directly from taskwise second moments;
finite-memory drift compares the replayed state with the state from
$q=\lambda$, normalized by the baseline state norm. A fixed sampled set of
parameter coordinates keeps storage bounded.

\subsection{Scale-estimation error and online feedback}
\label{app:score_approximation}

For positive costs and a proposal before flooring, define
$\widetilde q_i=q_ic_i/(\sum_jq_jc_j)$. Let $q^*$ use current all-task scales
and let $\widehat q$ use online estimates. Direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}
    \frac{J(\widehat q)}{J(q^*)}
    =1+D_{\chi^2}(\widetilde q^*\,\|\,\widetilde{\widehat q}).
    \label{eq:chi_square_gap}
\end{equation}
This identity diagnoses scale-estimation error at one fixed state. Online training moves
the reference scales, and the probability floor adds a separate allocation
cost. We log reference age and report the floor contribution separately.

\subsection{Strict curriculum decomposition}
\label{app:curriculum_decomposition}

An online curriculum can generate a different objective sequence after the
training path changes. Native D$^3$ first records its sequence
$\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_T$ for each seed. The paired D$^3$ target + GPAS run
replays that sequence and changes only $q_t$. Both runs share initialization,
per-task data order, and evaluation prompts.

D$^3$-Proposal-IC serves a different comparison. It keeps
$\lambda_i=1/4$, forms $q_t$ from the published loss-gap and descent-velocity
signal, and applies $\lambda_i/q_{t,i}$. Its comparison with GPAS measures two
proposal signals under one weighted teacher-loss objective.

\subsection{Sampler state, clipping, and resume}
\label{app:sampler_state}

Each checkpoint stores the scale average, runtime average, observation count,
scale age, probability floor, and current proposal for every task. The
optimizer checkpoint stores the usual AdamW state and a flag identifying the
second-moment rule. Resume tests compare the next proposal and optimizer state
before and after serialization.

The theory concerns the raw teacher-loss gradient. Training applies one shared
emergency clip before importance correction. This nonlinear map changes the
mean relative to the original loss gradient when clipping fires, while the
$\lambda/q$ correction stays unbiased for the clipped update field. Every
method reports clip rate by task, multiplier quantiles, corrected-update norm
quantiles, and the fraction of steps affected.
